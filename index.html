Research

Five questions we work on.

01 / 05

Margin visualization for lung-cancer surgery

Conventional ultrasound is challenging in lung tissue because alveoli scatter sound aggressively. Lung nodules contain no alveoli, so they scatter very differently — we exploit that contrast to detect and localize nodules in inflated lungs and measure the distance from the nodule edge to a surgical stapler. The goal: let surgeons verify the resection margin in real time, during minimally invasive surgery.

02 / 05

Lung tissue characterization

Ultrasound is a poor imaging modality in healthy lung — but a remarkable sensing modality. We extract physically meaningful properties from how waves propagate through millions of air-filled alveoli. In a rat model we showed that this can grade the severity of pulmonary fibrosis and pulmonary edema, with potential for point-of-care monitoring in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis or cardiogenic edema.

03 / 05

Complex porous media

In bone or metal foams, bulk material properties don't predict mechanical competence — the micro-architecture does. We build model-based ultrasonic methods that non-invasively, and in situ, recover micro-mechanical and micro-architectural properties of porous solids.

04 / 05

Microvasculature

Tumor vascular networks are denser, more isotropic, more tortuous, and more random than healthy ones. We develop methods grounded in wave propagation in complex media to characterize microvasculature non-invasively — aiming to improve the specificity of ultrasound for screening, diagnosing, and monitoring lesion malignancy.

05 / 05

Ultrasound stimulation of platelet-like particles

Platelet-like particles (PLPs) are highly deformable microgels designed to emulate platelet behavior. We're showing that ultrasound stimulation induces larger PLP deformations and accelerates wound healing — with potential applications for patients with diabetes.

Consortium

ULTRASONC — North Carolina's medical-ultrasound consortium.

The Muller Lab is part of ULTRASONC, a consortium of medical ultrasound investigators spanning Duke, NC A&T, NC State, UNC Chapel Hill, and UNC Charlotte. Our shared mission is to advance medical ultrasound technology and to train the next generation of ultrasound engineers.
