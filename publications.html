  • Quantitative Ultrasound to Stage and Monitor Interstitial Lung Diseases
    Active Grant National Institutes of Health (NIH) $2,650,000.00 2024 – 2029
    Investigators: Dr. Marie Muller (NC State MAE) & Dr. Jonathan Mamou (Albert Einstein College of Medicine)
    Funding Agency: National Institutes of Health (NIH)

    Developing non-invasive, radiation-free point-of-care quantitative ultrasound (QUS) biomarkers based on acoustic backscattering and multiple scattering physics to diagnose, stage, and track progression in interstitial lung diseases (ILD), offering a bedside alternative to repeated HRCT scans.

    • Develop QUS biomarkers sensitive to sub-wavelength parenchymal architectural remodeling.
    • Quantify disease severity in preclinical fibrotic and edematous lung models.
    • Translate point-of-care algorithms to portable handheld ultrasound scanners for bedside monitoring.
  • Quantitative Assessment of Angiogenesis using Ultrasound Multiple Scattering
    Active Grant National Institutes of Health (NIH) $2,328,411.00 06/15/2023 – 05/31/2028
    Investigators: Dr. Marie Muller (NC State University)
    Funding Agency: National Institutes of Health (NIH)

    Angiogenesis plays a critical role in cancer, atherosclerosis (vasa vasorum), and wound repair. While conventional Doppler and super-resolution ultrasound display vascular geometry, they fail to provide quantitative metrics. This project develops real-time assessment of microvascular architecture using Ultrasound Multiple Scattering (USMS) analysis of raw radio-frequency waveforms to establish biomarkers of tumor malignancy and plaque vulnerability.

    • Develop and optimize quantitative ultrasound methods to assess microvascular density and anisotropy non-invasively.
    • Establish proof-of-concept that multiple scattering differentiates tumor malignancy from benign tissue in preclinical models.
    • Validate diagnostic efficacy in preliminary clinical studies against standard point-of-care ultrasound.
  • Building AI Expertise to Transform Quantitative Ultrasound and Reveal Hidden Tissue Information
    Fellowship Award NC State College of Engineering $25,000.00 2026 – Present
    Investigators: Dr. Marie Muller
    Funding Agency: NC State College of Engineering

    Inaugural Dean's Applied AI Faculty Scholarly Fellowship project integrating physics-based acoustic scattering models with machine learning architectures to extract hidden microstructural tissue properties from raw ultrasonic radiofrequency signals.

  • Novel Ultrasonic Methods for the Assessment of Pulmonary Edema
    Completed Award National Institutes of Health (NIH) $430,285.00 09/01/2018 – 08/31/2021
    Investigators: Dr. Marie Muller (NC State MAE)
    Funding Agency: National Institutes of Health (NIH)

    Pulmonary edema is excessive fluid accumulation in alveolar spaces resulting from congestive heart failure or acute respiratory distress. Serial, radiation-free ultrasound assessments allow clinicians to evaluate progression and resolution of fluid overload directly at the point of care.

    • Formulate physics-based multiple scattering parameters to quantify lung fluid volume continuously.
    • Differentiate cardiogenic alveolar edema from sub-pleural fibrotic thickening.
    • Validate quantitative acoustic indices against gravimetric wet/dry lung weight ratios.
  • Novel Ultrasound Localization of Pulmonary Nodules during Video Assisted Thoracic Surgery to Improve Surgical Resection
    Completed Award National Institutes of Health (NIH / NCI) $362,210.00 05/15/2019 – 10/31/2022
    Investigators: Dr. Marie Muller (NC State) & Dr. Thomas M. Egan (UNC Surgery)
    Funding Agency: National Institutes of Health (NIH / NCI)

    Small, deep pulmonary nodules are often non-palpable and difficult to localize during minimally invasive Video-Assisted Thoracic Surgery (VATS), frequently forcing conversion to open thoracotomy. This project developed a specialized intraoperative continuum ultrasound probe that exploits acoustic multiple scattering to detect nodule boundaries in inflated lungs, guiding surgeons to achieve clean surgical margins in real time.

    • Design and test an actuated flexible continuum ultrasound probe suitable for VATS ports.
    • Differentiate dense neoplastic nodule tissue from highly scattering surrounding aerated lung parenchyma.
    • Accurately determine the margin distance from nodule edge to surgical stapler lines.
  • Innovative Ultrasonic Methods For The Diagnosis And Monitoring Of Pulmonary Fibrosis
    Completed Award National Institutes of Health (NIH) $438,815.00 04/01/2018 – 03/31/2021
    Investigators: Dr. Marie Muller (NC State MAE)
    Funding Agency: National Institutes of Health (NIH)

    Pulmonary fibrosis causes progressive alveolar destruction and dense collagen deposition. Standard pulmonary function tests detect functional loss only after irreversible architectural damage has occurred. This study developed acoustic diffusion metrics to detect microstructural remodeling long before macroscopic changes appear on CT.

  • Ultrasound Enhanced Platelet-like Particle Therapy for Accelerated Wound Repair
    Sponsored Research National Institutes of Health (NIH) / Collaborative $375,000.00 09/01/2018 – 08/31/2022
    Investigators: Dr. Marie Muller (NC State) & Dr. Ashley C. Brown (UNC / NC State Joint BME)
    Funding Agency: National Institutes of Health (NIH) / Collaborative

    Integrating deformable synthetic platelet-like microgels (PLPs) with acoustic radiation force to stimulate clot contraction, accelerate hemostasis, and promote tissue regeneration in acute trauma and surgical wound healing.

  • Ultrasonic Characterization of Atherosclerotic Plaque using Multiple Scattering
    Completed Award National Institutes of Health (NIH) $429,000.00 07/01/2017 – 06/30/2020
    Investigators: Dr. Marie Muller (Co-Investigator: Dr. Xiaoning Jiang)
    Funding Agency: National Institutes of Health (NIH)

    Rupture of vulnerable atherosclerotic plaques in carotid arteries is the primary cause of stroke. This study developed multi-element transducer arrays and backscattering signal processing algorithms to quantify the scattering mean free path in carotid plaques, directly correlating acoustic signatures with fibrous cap thickness and intraplaque hemorrhage.

  • Assessment of Bone Micro-Structure using Ultrasonic Methods
    Completed Award National Institutes of Health (NIH) $139,195.00 08/02/2016 – 05/31/2019
    Investigators: Dr. Marie Muller (NC State MAE)
    Funding Agency: National Institutes of Health (NIH)

    Osteoporosis alters both bone microarchitecture and density. Current clinical DXA assessment relies solely on bone mineral density using ionizing radiation. This research developed a non-ionizing quantitative ultrasound technique sensitive to cortical pore size, pore density, and anisotropy using multiple scattering principles.

    • Establish quantitative relationships between bone micro-architecture and multiple scattering parameters.
    • Validate wave propagation models in synthetic cortical bone phantoms and biological specimens.
    • Combine microstructural ultrasound metrics with mineral density for improved fracture risk prediction.
  • Experimental Ultrasonic Characterization of Bone Micro-Architecture

    Investigators: Dr. Marie Muller (NC State MAE)
    Funding Agency: NCSU Faculty Research and Professional Development

    Faculty seed grant supporting laboratory experimental validation of high-frequency broadband ultrasound transducers and custom synthetic phantoms mimicking healthy and osteoporotic cortical bone pores.

  • Quantitative Characterization of Trabecular Bone Micro-Architecture Using Ultrasound: Applications to Spaceflight Osteopenia
    Sponsored Award NC Space Grant Consortium / NASA $10,000.00 06/01/2016 – 05/31/2017
    Investigators: Dr. Marie Muller (NC State MAE)
    Funding Agency: NC Space Grant Consortium / NASA

    Astronauts experience rapid deterioration of trabecular micro-architecture during long-duration space missions. This project formulated radiation-free, portable ultrasonic monitoring protocols to assess microgravity-induced skeletal disuse and evaluate the efficacy of orbital exercise countermeasures.
